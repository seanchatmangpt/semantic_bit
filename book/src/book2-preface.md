# Book 2 — 8-Bit Semantic Multiplexing

The first book established the foundation: the semantic bit. We learned that a named position in a bounded field carries an admitted operational meaning. We learned that activation may be many, but selection must be one.

In this second book, we widen the carrier without losing the discipline.

We move from the individual access field to the **Semantic Byte**. The byte is the fundamental unit of machine memory, but in this series, it is the fundamental unit of machine meaning. An 8-bit field is not merely a collection of flags; it is a multiplexed carrier capable of representing complex operational states, nouns, verbs, and relations while remaining small enough to be understood, verified, and preserved.

The transition from a bit to a byte is not a change in philosophy. It is an expansion of the field.

We will explore how eight bits can be multiplexed to represent:
- **Status8**: The admitted truth of a field's state.
- **ConditionCode8**: The selected outcome of an operational decision.
- **Operation64**: The combination of noun and verb into a single executable cell.
- **Relation64**: The bounded connection between two identities.
- **COG8**: The closed cognitive field of an agent's understanding.
- **CONSTRUCT8**: The admitted construction of new meaning.

The discipline remains the same: **Meaning Before Motion.**

The system does not act because it "feels" it is time. It acts because the field has been admitted, the condition has been selected, and the receipt has been preserved.
