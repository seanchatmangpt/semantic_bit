# Eight Verbs

If the Eight Nouns bound the *targets* of action, the **Eight Verbs** bound the *forms* of action. 

In a traditional programming language, you might have `create`, `update`, `delete`, `fetch`, `merge`, `sync`, `validate`, and dozens of other unstructured verbs scattered across APIs. In Operation64, we reduce all operational motion to exactly eight fundamental verbs.

A canonical set of verbs in the Semantic8 Spine includes:

1. **ACTIVATE**: To assert a bit into a field, making a meaning active.
2. **SELECT**: To apply a rule to a field to yield a single condition.
3. **DEPOSIT**: To place a record into a channel or journal.
4. **REVOKE**: To remove an authority or invalidate a relation.
5. **REPLAY**: To re-evaluate a receipt as if it were a new field.
6. **BIND**: To link two nouns together, forming a relation.
7. **EMIT**: To generate a new receipt and close the current transaction.
8. **HALT**: To completely stop the processing of the current channel.

Every complex process in the system must be decomposed into a sequence of these eight verbs. There is no `compute_complex_thing`. There is only the activation of fields, the selection of conditions, the binding of relations, and the emission of receipts.

This strict vocabulary ensures that every action is legible. When an operator or an auditor looks at an operation code, they know exactly what kind of motion is occurring, without having to read the underlying source code.
