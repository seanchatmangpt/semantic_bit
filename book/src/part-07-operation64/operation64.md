# Operation64

Once a condition is resolved—once the noise of active statuses has been funneled through a selection rule into a single, unambiguous `ConditionCode8`—the system must act. But action in a semantic system cannot be an unbounded string or a free-form function call. Action, like state, must be bounded, deterministic, and receiptable.

This is the domain of **Operation64**.

Operation64 is the third vertebra of the Semantic8 Spine. It answers the question: *Given this condition, what are we authorized to do?* 

To ensure that the space of possible actions remains finite and strictly measurable, Operation64 defines exactly 64 possible operational cells. It does this not by listing 64 arbitrary actions, but by combining two fundamental axes: **Eight Nouns** and **Eight Verbs**.

In this part, we will explore:
1. How eight nouns bound the targets of action.
2. How eight verbs bound the forms of action.
3. How their intersection creates exactly 64 declared operation cells.
4. How an operation is selected, authorized, and finally receipted.

By bounding operations in this way, we eliminate the ambiguity of "doing things" in software. There are no hidden side effects; there are only 64 known operations, and every execution leaves a cryptographic trail of exactly what was attempted and why.
