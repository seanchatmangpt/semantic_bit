# After Multiplexing

The second book exhausted the possibilities of the 8-bit multiplexed field. We have learned to bound state, select conditions, and emit receipts. We have learned that eight bits are enough to govern the motion of a single machine.

But a system is more than a single machine. It is a collection of machines, identities, and histories that must relate to one another.

$$
\boxed{\textbf{Multiplexing governs the cell. Relations govern the system.}}
$$

## 15.1 The Limit of the Byte

The Semantic Byte is a closed world. It admits only what it can carry in its 8 positions. It is excellent for "Status" or "Condition", but it is silent about "Identity" and "Context". 

If Machine A is `OK` and Machine B is `BLOCKED`, the 8-bit fields tell us the state of each. But they do not tell us *why* Machine B is blocked by Machine A. That meaning exists *between* the fields.

## 15.2 The Third Book

In this third book, we move beyond the bit and the byte. We introduce the **Triple** and the **Graph**. 

We do not abandon the bit. We use the bit to admit the triple. We use the triple to name the bit. 

The journey from "Meaning Before Motion" continues. We now seek "Relation Before Action".
