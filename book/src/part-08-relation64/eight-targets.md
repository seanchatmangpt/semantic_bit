# Eight Targets

Just as the origin of a relation must be classified, so too must its destination. The end of the directed edge must resolve to one of **Eight Targets**.

The Eight Targets classify the *destination* of the relationship. They mirror the Eight Sources, creating a closed loop of interaction:

1. **SUBJECT**: An active entity.
2. **OBJECT**: A passive data structure.
3. **EVENT**: A discrete occurrence.
4. **RULE**: A static definition.
5. **LOCUS**: A boundary or container.
6. **CLAIM**: An unverified assertion.
7. **PROOF**: Cryptographic evidence.
8. **STATE**: A persistent condition.

By enforcing that both the beginning and the end of every connection fall into one of eight categories, we ensure that the entire graph of relationships can be analyzed at a macroscopic level without needing to inspect the underlying strings or payloads. We know immediately if an `EVENT` points to a `SUBJECT`, or if a `PROOF` points to a `RULE`.
